EcoEarn · plastic bottle machine

How to build the EcoEarn machine

A step-by-step guide for the group. It shows what each part does, where it goes, how big to make it, and how to wire it to the ESP32. Read the whole thing once before you buy or cut anything.

What already works

The card reader (RFID), the scale (load cell + HX711), the ESP32 program and the EcoEarn app are done and tested. You are building the body of the machine and the moving parts. When you finish each part, tell the developer so the program can be switched from "pretend" to "real" for that part.

Where the laptop and your devices go

Right now you have the ESP32, the card reader on the breadboard, the HX711 and the load cell, all joined with jumper wires, plus the laptop. This is how to place them in the machine today, before the motors and the other sensors arrive.

Why the breadboard goes right behind the card reader

The card reader's wires carry fast signals and only work when short, so the reader and the breadboard must be within your 20 cm jumpers. The HX711's signals are slow, so its wires can run about a meter down to the scale without problems.

Steps for today

  1. Take the card reader off the breadboard

    It is plugged straight into the breadboard now, but it has to sit on the front panel. First take a clear photo. Write down which breadboard row each of its pins is in. Then pull it out and connect its pins to the same rows with 7 male-to-female jumpers. IRQ stays unused.

    Tap a card in the app's Live screen. It should still show the card number.

  2. Mount the card reader

    Tape or screw it flat behind the acrylic window, the side with the printed coil facing out. Keep metal away from its front.

  3. Mount the board with the breadboard and ESP32

    Stick the breadboard onto a small plywood board, tape the ESP32 down beside it, put foam under the board, and screw it into the compartment right behind the card reader.

  4. Run the HX711 wires down to the scale

    Screw the HX711 onto the scale shelf next to the load cell, so the load cell's 4 colored wires stay short. For its 4 pins (GND, DT, SCK, VCC) to the breadboard, use a 4-core cable about 1 m long. Old Ethernet (LAN) cable works well: it has 8 thin wires inside. Solder a jumper end onto each wire and cover it with heat-shrink or tape. If there is no time, join jumpers end to end and tape each joint.

    Put coins on the tray 3 times. The weight should be the same each time.

  5. Build the laptop shelf

    A plywood shelf on the side of the machine, about 85 cm from the floor, with a small lip at the front edge so the laptop can't slide off. Put it on the side away from the drain hose.

  6. Connect the laptop

    Run the USB cable from the laptop through a hole in the machine wall to the ESP32. Zip-tie it inside so a pull on the cable doesn't reach the ESP32. Plug in the laptop's charger.

  7. Start display mode

    Sign in to EcoEarn as admin, then click the TV button in the sidebar. The laptop becomes the machine's screen and connects to the ESP32 by itself.

Stop the laptop from sleeping

On the laptop: Windows Settings → System → Power → Screen and sleep → set "When plugged in, turn off my screen after" and "put my device to sleep after" to Never. If it sleeps, the machine stops recording deposits.

Section 1

How the machine works

One bottle at a time goes through these steps. The ESP32 does them in this order and the screen shows each one.

1Tap cardThe student taps their ID card on the reader. The scale zeroes itself.
2Put bottle inBottle goes into the chamber. The IR sensor sees it. The lid switch confirms the lid is closed.
3CrushThe actuator pushes the plate down. Spikes pierce the bottle and the plate flattens it.
4DrainThe plate goes back up. For 5 seconds, leftover liquid runs out of the drain hole.
5DropThe trapdoor servo opens. The flat bottle falls down the chute onto the scale tray.
6WeighThe scale waits for a steady reading and computes points (default 1 point per 10 g).
7Into the binThe sweeper servo pushes the bottle off the tray into the storage bin.
8Check binThe ultrasonic sensor measures how full the bin is. At 90% the machine refuses bottles.

Do not use a single-channel relay for the actuator

A one-channel relay can only turn the actuator on in one direction, so it could crush but never pull back up. The BTS7960 drives it both ways and lets the program control it safely.

Section 3

Safety rules (read these first)

The actuator pushes with 1500 N, about the weight of 150 kg.

It can break fingers. Never put your hand in the chamber while the machine has power.

  • Unplug the 12 V power before touching any wire. Also unplug the USB cable before moving wires on the ESP32.
  • 12 V must never touch the ESP32. The ESP32 only gets 5 V from a buck converter. One wrong wire destroys it.
  • Set each buck converter's voltage with a multimeter before connecting anything to it. They often come set to the wrong voltage.
  • The RFID reader runs on 3.3 V only, never 5 V.
  • The lid must close fully over the chamber. The program will not move anything when the lid is open.
  • Put the emergency stop button where a person standing at the machine can hit it.
  • Keep water away from the electronics box. Liquid only goes out through the drain hose.
  • Every connection on the 12 V side must be screwed into a terminal or soldered and covered with heat-shrink. No bare wire.
Section 4

Build the body and the crushing chamber

The frame must be strong. When the actuator pushes down on the bottle, it pushes up on the top crossbar with the same force. A weak top will bend or crack.

Sizes to aim for

  • Chamber inside: 110 × 110 mm, 260 mm tall.
  • Whole machine: about 45 cm wide, 40 cm deep, 115 cm tall.
  • The actuator body is about 310 mm long when pulled in. It sits above the chamber.
  • With a 200 mm stroke, the plate stops about 30 mm above the trapdoor. That is the flat bottle's final thickness.

Measure your actuator when it arrives. If its length is different, adjust the frame height, not the chamber.

  1. Build the frame

    Make a rectangular frame from steel square tube or angle bar. Weld or bolt the corners. Add a strong crossbar at the top where the actuator will hang. Add a shelf about 25 cm from the floor for the scale.

  2. Build the chamber

    Make a box with 4 walls and an open top and bottom, 110 × 110 mm inside, 260 mm tall. Use steel sheet, or 18 mm plywood lined with metal sheet. Plywood alone gets wet and swells.

  3. Make the lid

    Cut a door on the front of the chamber, big enough to drop a bottle in (about 100 × 240 mm). Hinge it on one side. A clear acrylic window lets students watch the crushing.

    The lid must close flat and stay closed. Add a small magnet or latch.

  4. Mount the chamber to the frame

    Bolt the chamber walls to the frame so the top is centered under the top crossbar. Leave room under the chamber for the trapdoor and chute (about 6 cm).

Section 5

The crusher: actuator, plate and spikes

  1. Hang the actuator

    Bolt the actuator's back end (the end with the motor) to the top crossbar with its mounting bracket and a steel pin. It must point straight down, centered over the chamber.

  2. Make the pusher plate

    Cut a 100 × 100 mm steel plate (5 mm thick). It must slide inside the 110 mm chamber with about 5 mm space on each side. Weld or bolt a short tube on top so it attaches to the actuator's rod end with a pin.

  3. Add the spikes

    Grind the tips of 4 M8 bolts to a point. Drill 4 holes in the plate, 25 mm in from each corner, and bolt the spikes through so 20 mm sticks out underneath. Tighten with nuts on top.

    The spikes punch holes in the bottle so the liquid can drain out and the bottle flattens easily.

  4. Check the travel

    Before wiring it to the ESP32, test the actuator straight on 12 V (red to +, black to −, then swapped). Watch it go all the way out and back.

    At full extension the plate must stop above the trapdoor, never hit it. Time how long it takes to go out and back. Tell the developer that number.

Why use an actuator with built-in limit switches?

Those switches stop the motor at each end by themselves. Even if the program had a problem, the actuator would not keep pushing past its end.

Section 6

Trapdoor and drain

The trapdoor is the floor of the chamber. The bottle gets crushed against it, then it swings open to drop the bottle. A servo is far too weak to hold 1500 N, so the door must rest on steel ledges while crushing. The servo only swings it open and closed.

  1. Weld the ledges

    Weld or bolt two strips of steel angle (15 mm) inside the bottom of the chamber, on the hinge side and the opposite side. The door rests on them when closed.

  2. Hinge the door

    Use a strong steel hinge (or a steel rod through two tubes) on one side. The door must swing down freely.

  3. Connect the servo

    Mount the MG996R on the outside of the chamber, next to the hinge. Connect its arm to the door with a short stiff wire or rod. The servo pulls the door shut and lets it swing open.

    With the door closed, push down on it hard with your hand. The ledges should carry it, and the servo arm should not move.

  4. Drill the drain hole

    Drill a 10 mm hole in the chamber wall, just above the closed door. Tilt the door very slightly toward that side (2–3 mm lower) so liquid flows to the hole.

  5. Attach the hose

    Push a 10 mm hose onto a short pipe fitting at the hole. Run it down to a closed container on the floor, away from the scale.

    Pour a cup of water into the chamber. All of it should come out of the hose, none on the scale.

  6. Add the chute

    Under the trapdoor, make a small funnel from sheet metal or plastic that guides the falling bottle to the middle of the scale tray. It must not touch the tray.

Section 7

Weighing station and sweeper

The scale already works on the bench. Mounting it wrong is the most common way to ruin the readings. Follow the picture closely.

  1. Mount the load cell

    Screw the fixed end firmly onto a wooden or metal block on the shelf. Use the 2 screw holes on that end only. Put a spacer on the free end's 2 holes and screw the tray onto the spacer.

    Nothing touches the load cell except the block and the spacer. Wires hang loose, not pulled tight.

  2. Keep the tray light and level

    Use a light tray about 120 × 120 mm with a low rim on 3 sides. Leave the bin side open so the bottle can be pushed off.

  3. Mount the sweeper servo

    Fix the second MG996R on the shelf (not on the tray!) at the back of the tray. Screw a flat arm, about 12 cm long, onto it. When it turns, the arm sweeps across the tray and pushes the bottle off the open side.

    At rest, the arm must not touch the tray or the bottle. Anything touching the tray changes the weight.

  4. Make a slide to the bin

    A short sloped plate from the tray's open edge into the bin, so the pushed bottle slides in.

  5. Recalibrate after mounting

    Once the scale is in the machine, open the EcoEarn app, go to Live → scale button, press Tare with the tray empty, then put a known weight (for example 3 coins weighed on a kitchen scale) and press Calibrate.

Section 8

Storage bin and fill sensor

  1. Place the bin

    Put the bin under the end of the slide. Make a door on the machine's side or front so the bin can be pulled out and emptied.

  2. Mount the ultrasonic sensor

    Fix the HC-SR04 above the bin, facing straight down, with its two "eyes" pointing at the bin floor. Nothing should be between the sensor and the bottom except the bottles.

    Measure the distance from the sensor to the empty bin floor in cm. Tell the developer. The program uses it to work out the fill percent.

Section 9

Front panel: card reader, screen, lid

Card reader

Mount the RFID reader behind a thin plastic or acrylic panel (max 5 mm), with its flat side facing out. Draw a card symbol on the front so students know where to tap.

Metal blocks the card reader

The reader cannot read through a steel panel. Cut a window in the metal and cover it with plastic, or mount the reader on a plastic part of the front.

Screen

Cut a rectangular window for the 20x4 LCD and screw it in from behind.

Lid switch

Mount the limit switch on the frame so the closing lid presses its lever. You should hear a click right when the lid closes fully, not before.

IR sensor

Drill a hole in the chamber wall about 3 cm above the trapdoor and screw the E18-D80NK in, pointing across the chamber. Turn the small screw on its back until its light turns on only when a bottle is inside.

Section 10

Power

One 12 V supply feeds everything. Two buck converters step it down for the parts that need less.

  1. Set the buck converters first

    Connect only the 12 V input to a buck converter. Measure its output with a multimeter and turn the small brass screw until it reads 5.0 V (Buck A) or 6.0 V (Buck B). Do this before connecting anything to the output.

  2. Wire the motor side

    12 V + → fuse → emergency stop → BTS7960 B+. 12 V − → BTS7960 B−. Actuator wires → M+ and M−.

    Use thick wire (18 AWG or bigger) here. The actuator can draw several amps.

  3. Power the ESP32 from Buck A

    Buck A + → ESP32 VIN. Buck A − → ESP32 GND. Never connect the 12 V supply to VIN.

    The laptop or Raspberry Pi USB cable can stay plugged in at the same time. That is fine.

  4. Join all grounds

    Every − wire (12 V supply, both bucks, BTS7960, servos, sensors, ESP32 GND) connects together on one terminal block. Without a shared ground the signals don't work.
